# Daily sports research (runs 09:00 Europe/Dublin)

Goal: a research page for every competitive sport with events in the next 24h. Free model predictions only, NO bookmaker odds. Times in Irish time.

1. **Setup.** `git pull origin claude/wonderful-cori-vxwyn9`; `pip install -q pytest tzdata`; `python3 -m pytest -q` must pass.
2. **Collect.** `python3 -m collect.run fixtures.json` fetches live-footballontv + Predicd (football), ESPN (NHL, NFL, NBA, WNBA, MLB, NCAA, tennis, rugby, MMA, golf), Covers/Odds Shark picks (predicted scores) and SetProb tennis win probabilities, merges them, aligns Predicd's time zone against the listings, and records every blocked or missing source under `skipped`. SetProb is read from its own JSON feed (`setprobability.pages.dev/api/fixtures`, tours atp, wta, atp-challenger, wta-challenger; falls back to the ATP list in its HTML) and matched to ESPN tennis fixtures by surname within 12 h; SetProb matches that ESPN does not list (e.g. ATP Challengers) are added as SetProb-sourced fixtures, never duplicated. The SetProb feed also carries betting-market odds: NEVER collect or show them. It also checks Flashscore (www.flashscore.com day feed for today and tomorrow, `x-fsign` header; match pages are fetched following redirects) for every modelled football fixture: where Flashscore has a written preview it records the author and the market Flashscore's writer picked in the 'Betting Analysis' paragraph (market and selection only, never prices) as `fixture['preview']`, and saves the preview sections to `out/previews_raw.json` (not committed). If the Flashscore feed or a match page is blocked or fails, the run says so under `skipped` and carries on without previews for it (say which in the final message); never fake a preview, pick or star. It never invents fixtures.
3. **Fill gaps (optional, only with data you actually fetched).** Cricket, darts, snooker, boxing, GAA, horse/greyhound racing, esports are not collected yet. If you can fetch a reliable source showing fixtures inside the window, append them to `fixtures.json` in the same schema (kickoff in ISO-8601 with offset, `models: []` unless a free source publishes a prediction) and remove that sport's "no collector yet" note. Otherwise leave the note. Do not use paywalled or stale data as current, and do not collect bookmaker odds.
3a. **Near-duplicate check** (after collecting, before building): pairs within 15 min of kickoff where one side has a model and the other not, listing those whose best team-name similarity (`collect.football._sim`) is above 0.55; each hit needs a glance (most are different games). A true duplicate pair means adding an alias in `collect.football.ALIASES` (with a test) before publishing.
3b. **Flashscore synopses (do this yourself in this session, every day, after the last collect and before building; subagents have stalled before).** Read `out/previews_raw.json`. For EVERY preview found, write an entry in `previews/YYYY-MM-DD.json` keyed by the Flashscore match id: `{"strengths": {"home": "...", "away": "..."}, "synopsis": "..."}`. `strengths` = each team's main strengths and weaknesses (form, key players, injuries/suspensions, home/away record) with no team-name prefix (the page adds it); `synopsis` = 1-2 sentences on how the match looks and what Flashscore's writer leans towards. Rules: use only facts in the preview text, in your own words (no long quotes), at most about 600 characters each, NO bookmaker odds or prices (the pick's market name is fine), never invent. Keep each field short (about 300-400 characters is plenty; big Saturdays can have 50+ previews). Then run `python3 -m collect.synopses fixtures.json previews/YYYY-MM-DD.json`; it prints 'synopses attached to N of M previews' (N should equal M). Commit the `previews/` file. Re-running `collect.run` rewrites `fixtures.json` and drops the synopses, so after any re-collect run `collect.synopses` again (the previews file is keyed by match id and can be reused). A preview with no recognised market simply has no pick and no star; if the pick parser misses a market that is clearly there (the `Betting Analysis` text is in the raw file), add the pattern to `collect/flashscore.py` `parse_pick` with a test.
4. **Build.** `python3 -m src.build fixtures.json out/index.html` (non-zero exit = quality gate failed; the page carries a WARNING banner, publish it anyway and say why). The build keeps ONLY fixtures that carry at least one model prediction; fixtures with no model are dropped from the page and the page says how many (`hidden` in the JSON result). Do not add them back by hand, and do not pass `models_only=False`. The page ('Layout v5') has:
   - BOLD in the Match column means the model favourite and nothing else (`src/score.py::favourite`: the side the model(s) favour; no bold for a draw/tie/level, when the models disagree, or when there is no model). Never bold the first-listed or home side by default, because bold reads as the prediction. The page says 'Bold = the model favourite' in the top bar;
   - a Sort select, a status line and the 'Layout v5' stamp at the top; the nav chips and every section heading show 'shown of total modelled' (e.g. 'Tennis 5 of 26'), updating live, so a filter never hides how many modelled fixtures exist;
   - a margin filter directly above each table: football default 2 goals, NHL 0.75 goals, NFL 6.5 points, and tennis a win-probability GAP (favourite % minus the other player's %, in percentage points) default 50, as a 0-100 slider linked to a number box. Football scores are DERIVED from Predicd's win probabilities (`src/score.py`, dashed box, labelled derived); NHL/NFL scores are Odds Shark's published scores (solid box); tennis rows show the favourite's win % in the box and no score. Other sports are not filtered;
   - football CUP games (domestic cups: Cup, Copa, Pokal, Coppa, Coupe, Taça, Beker, Trophy; not Champions/Europa/Conference League, Nations League or World Cup; `src/cups.py`) in their own table inside a collapsed 'Cup games' accordion under the football table, because favourites often rotate or throw cup ties; the football margin filter applies to it too and its header shows 'N of M shown';
   - a Flashscore block in each football row that has a preview: the Flashscore pick (market + selection), a 'Preview synopsis' dropdown (your strengths/synopsis, labelled as written by Claude, with the author and a link to the full preview), and a gold STAR (also a '★ Flashscore pick matches Predicd' pill) when the pick covers Predicd's most likely result (`src/preview.py`); a '★ Flashscore agrees with Predicd' strip near the top lists every starred match whatever the filters say;
   - EVERY model on a fixture in its Model predictions cell (probability bar, or the score for score-only models), 'single source, low confidence' when there is one, a disagreement flag when 2+ models differ by 5+ pp; for tennis, SetProb's per-factor breakdown (Elo, H2H, last 10, surface) under its bar, which are SetProb's own components, not separate models.
   All modelled rows stay in the HTML; the default thresholds are applied server-side (hidden rows carry class `off`), rows are sorted by margin descending, and the controls re-filter/re-sort in the browser. Fixtures stay in `fixtures.json` and `fixtures/YYYY-MM-DD.json` for the record, then create `out/artifact.html` with `artifact_fragment` from `src/render.py`.
4b. **Self-check.** `python3 -m src.build` already adds a WARNING if the score column, score boxes or margin controls (football, NHL, NFL, tennis) are missing (`validate.check_page`). If you change the page layout, bump the stamp ('Layout vN' in `src/render.py`, plus the tests) so a stale cached copy is easy to spot. After publishing, tell the user the stamp ('Layout v5'). In every build, before publishing, check that in each row the bolded name is the side the model favours (the Predicd top outcome, the higher win %, or the higher score) and that no row is bolded when it is a draw or the models disagree. Also, optional but worthwhile after any page change: open `out/index.html` in headless Chromium (Playwright is installed) and check there are no script errors, the filters work, the Cup games accordion opens and the STAR strip lists the starred matches.
5. **Publish** `out/artifact.html` as an Artifact, updating the SAME single URL recorded in CLAUDE.md (read it first, then publish with `url`). There is only one artifact now; do not publish a second copy.
6. **Save.** After the synopses are applied and the page is built and published, copy `fixtures.json` to `fixtures/YYYY-MM-DD.json` (it then carries the previews and synopses), commit it together with `previews/YYYY-MM-DD.json`, and push to `claude/wonderful-cori-vxwyn9`. Add the day's run-log line to CLAUDE.md (counts per sport, modelled/shown/hidden, Flashscore previews found and STARs, near-duplicate hits, anything blocked) plus new learnings (blocked hosts, layout changes, parser fixes). Do not commit `out/`.
7. **Final message** (this is what the user reads): first line = artifact link. Then at most 4 lines: stale/missing/blocked sources, and the 2-3 most notable model disagreements (model vs model; there is no bookmaker comparison; say so when every model is single-source). Also say how many Flashscore previews were found and how many matches carry the STAR (Flashscore agrees with Predicd), naming them. Also give the counts: modelled fixtures in total (with the per-sport split, e.g. tennis 28, football 15), how many are shown at the default filters, and how many were hidden for having no model. Max ~5 lines.
